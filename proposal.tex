\subsection*{Problem Statement}
The bandwidth of Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) 6.0 is too low to support transmission 
of raw neural extracellular electrophysiology data (hereby referred to as continuous neural data) 
sampled at the industry standard from more than two recording channels at once for offline spike 
sorting and consequent neuroscientific analysis [insert reference].

% ======= Need ======= %
\subsection*{Need Derived from Problem Statement}
Compress continuous neural data on the head stage of a rodent without domain specific 
loss of fidelity for wireless transmission. 

% ======= Design Requirements and Technical Specifications ======= %
\section*{Design Requirements and Technical Specifications}
\subsection*{Description}

The proposed system consists of two overarching parts, or phases, under different 
constraints: one offline phase and one online phase. The offline phase runs once 
on a workstation where it sorts a reference recording and trains the classifier. The 
online phase, running on the SoC, performs inference against the resulting pre-trained 
model. Only the online phase is subject to the power, latency, and memory constraints that
motivate this thesis. The requirements described below apply exclusively to the
online phase unless otherwise noted.

The online phase realizes the declared need through five procedural operations: it
\emph{acquires} extracellular voltage from multiple recording channels,
\emph{detects} spike events within the acquired signal, \emph{records} the time
at which each detected spike occurs, \emph{classifies} each spike by assigning
it a unit identity, and \emph{transmits} each unit identity, paired with its
timestamp, over a wireless link. These five operations constitute the
functional requirements of the online system. However, despite realizing the declared 
need,the operations making up the functional requirements of the online phase are 
dependent on the offline phase.

Technical requirements were first derived independently for each stage of
this online pipeline. One requirement, however, resisted this per-stage
decomposition: that no spike be lost. A sample or detected spike dropped
anywhere in the chain --- whether during acquisition, transmission to the
classifier, or over the wireless link --- compromises the final result
identically. This requirement is therefore treated as a system-level
requirement spanning the full pipeline, summarized separately in Table 2,
rather than attached to any single functional requirement in Table 1.
