SPI link

nRF54LM20B (controller) ↔ Intan RHD2164 (peripheral)

Every sample is one 16-bit frame. The nRF pulls CS low and clocks 16 pulses on SCLK at 16 MHz. It shifts a CONVERT(channel) command out on MOSI, while the Intan shifts back on MISO the result of the command sent two frames earlier. CS rising ends the frame, and its falling edge starts the next conversion.

Burst scheduling

Each channel is sampled once per sample period, every 50 µs at 20 kS/s. The frames for the different channels can be arranged in two ways. With even spacing, one frame starts every 12.5 µs, so the SPI lines switch throughout the period. With burst scheduling, all frames run back to back at the start of the period, 2 µs apart, and the bus stays idle for the remaining 43 µs. The firmware uses burst scheduling.

On the jumper-wire bench at 4 channels × 20 kS/s, spikes are lost in noise with even spacing and clearly resolved on every channel with burst scheduling.

Classification

On-chip spike classification with a 1-D CNN on the nRF54LM20B

Each detected spike is cut out as a short window and passed through a small convolutional network. Its convolution layers learn the waveform features that tell neurons apart, such as trough depth, width and repolarisation. A dense layer and softmax then assign the spike to a unit. The headstage then sends which unit fired and when instead of the waveform, so far more channels fit over the same radio link.

Intan RHD2164

64-channel amplifier and 16-bit ADC (U2)

Each electrode gets its own low-noise amplifier and band-pass filter. A multiplexer then feeds the channels one at a time into a single 16-bit ADC. Every SPI frame converts one channel, so the chip's total conversion rate is channels × sample rate.

On the jumper-wire bench, noise grew with that total rate: 86 µV at 1 channel and 232 µV at 10 channels, both at 20 kS/s, against a datasheet input noise of 2.4 µV. Short traces and a clean supply on this board are meant to close that gap.

Battery

Grepow GRP3014025 LiPo on a JST SH 2-pin connector (J1)

A single LiPo cell powers everything through two regulators. The cell runs from 4.2 V full to about 3.0 V empty. The Intan needs at least 3.2 V and the regulators drop about 0.1 V, so the board stops near 3.4 V and the last part of the charge goes unused.

A 1 MΩ / 1 MΩ divider lets the nRF read the battery at half scale on P1.31/AIN1, costing about 2 µA.

Power

Two LP5907MFX-3.3 low-noise regulators (U3, U4)

The Intan and the nRF each get their own regulator. The radio draws current in bursts at every connection event. On a shared rail those bursts would ripple straight into the Intan's supply, right next to the electrode inputs. Separate very-low-noise LDOs keep the analog rail quiet.

Antenna

Johanson 2450AT18A100 chip antenna, 2.4 GHz Bluetooth LE (AE1)

The nRF's radio pin drives the chip antenna through a five-part LC ladder that matches the chip to the antenna. The headstage streams to a second nRF over Bluetooth LE 2M PHY. That link carries about 1.05 Mbit/s, while a Mac as the receiver tops out near 470 kbit/s, which is why the relay exists.

Clocks

32 MHz (X1) and 32.768 kHz (X2) crystals

The 32 MHz crystal is the radio's frequency reference. Bluetooth needs its accuracy to stay on channel, so it is required. The 32.768 kHz crystal keeps time while the chip sleeps between radio events. Without it, the nRF falls back to a less accurate internal oscillator, which costs power. The nRF54 has internal load capacitors for both, so no external capacitors are needed.

nRF power stage

On-chip DC/DC converter and internal regulator outputs

Inside the nRF, a switching DC/DC converter steps the 3.3 V supply down for the chip's core, which cuts the current the radio and CPU draw from the battery. It needs the external 4.7 µH inductor L1 on the DCC pin. The chip also brings out decoupling pins for its digital (DECD), analog (DECA) and radio (DECRF) supplies, each filtered the way Nordic's reference design shows.

Programming

10-pin 1.27 mm SWD header (J3) and status LED (D1)

J3 is the standard 10-pin ARM Cortex debug header. The nRF54LM20 DK programs external boards from its Debug out header over the same kind of 10-pin ribbon cable, so it plugs straight in and the standard build-and-flash flow works unchanged. A green LED on P1.00 gives a heartbeat without a serial port.
