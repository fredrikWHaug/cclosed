SPI link

nRF54LM20B (master) ↔ Intan RHD2164 (slave)

Every sample is one 16-bit frame. The nRF pulls CS low and clocks 16 pulses on SCLK at 16 MHz. It shifts a CONVERT(channel) command out on MOSI, while the Intan shifts back on MISO the result of the command sent two frames earlier. CS rising ends the frame, and its falling edge starts the next conversion.

Clock
16 MHz, 62.5 ns per bit
Frame
16 bits, about 1 µs with CS setup and hold
Timing
hardware timer → DPPI → SPIM, 0.38 µs jitter
Signalling
CMOS, LVDS_en tied to ground
Pins
SCLK P2.01 · MOSI P2.02 · MISO P2.04 · CS P2.05
On this board
33 Ω series resistors R1 to R4, short traces

Classification

On-chip spike classification with a 1-D CNN on the nRF54LM20B

Each detected spike is cut out as a short window and passed through a small convolutional network. Its convolution layers learn the waveform features that tell neurons apart, such as trough depth, width and repolarisation. A dense layer and softmax then assign the spike to a unit. The headstage then sends which unit fired and when instead of the waveform, so far more channels fit over the same radio link.

Input
61-sample window, 2 ms at 30 kS/s, from the on-chip 4σ detector
Model
2 × Conv1D → pooling → dense → softmax
Hardware
Axon NPU in the nRF54LM20B
Output
unit ID + sample timestamp
Per spike on air
about 8 bytes instead of a 101-byte window, roughly 12× less
Today
offline nearest-template classifier, 98–100% agreement with the sorter on FB128 data
